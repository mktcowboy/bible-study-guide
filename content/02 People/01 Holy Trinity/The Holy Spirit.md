# The Holy Spirit

## Overview
- **Testament:** Both — active from the first verse of Genesis to the last chapter of Revelation
- **Names:** the Spirit of God / Spirit of the LORD (Genesis 1:2; Isaiah 61:1); the Holy Ghost / Holy Spirit (Luke 1:15; Acts 1:8); the Comforter / Advocate — Greek *Parakletos* (John 14:16, 26); the Spirit of Truth (John 14:17); the Spirit of Christ (Romans 8:9); the Spirit of adoption (Romans 8:15); the Spirit of grace (Hebrews 10:29); the eternal Spirit (Hebrews 9:14)
- **Nature:** the third person of the Godhead — fully God, fully personal, proceeding from the Father and sent by the Son; not an impersonal force or influence but "he," who teaches, intercedes, grieves, and can be lied to
- **Role:** agent of creation, inspiration, regeneration, indwelling, sanctification, empowerment for witness, and the guarantee of the resurrection to come
- **Key Books:** [[Genesis]], [[Judges]], [[Isaiah]], [[Ezekiel]], [[Joel]], [[Luke]], [[John]], [[Acts]], [[Romans]], [[1 Corinthians]], [[Galatians]], [[Ephesians]]

The Holy Spirit is God himself, present and active — not a lesser emanation of God and not merely his effect on the world. Where [[God]] the Father is the source of all things and [[Jesus Christ]] the Son is the one through whom all things were made, the Spirit is the one by whom the Father and the Son are present and at work in creation, in Scripture, and in the hearts of believers. He does not typically draw attention to himself; his characteristic ministry is to glorify the Son (John 16:14) and conform believers to the Father's will. This has sometimes been called his "shy" character in historic theology — not because he is any less fully God, but because his mission is to point beyond himself.

---

## In Creation and the Old Testament

"And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters" (Genesis 1:2). Before the first word of creation was spoken, the Spirit was present, hovering over the formless deep — the same verb used of a bird hovering over its nest (Deuteronomy 32:11). Job affirms the same: "By his spirit he hath garnished the heavens" (Job 26:13), and "The spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life" (Job 33:4). Creation itself, and the life of every creature, is Spirit-given and Spirit-sustained: "Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth" (Psalm 104:30).

In the Old Testament the Spirit comes upon specific people for specific tasks, typically as an empowering rather than a permanent indwelling. He filled [[Bezalel]] with "wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship" to craft the tabernacle (Exodus 31:3-5). He came mightily upon the judges — [[Othniel]], [[Gideon]], [[Jephthah]], and especially [[Samson]], upon whom the Spirit moved him at times (Judges 13:25) and came mightily so that he tore a lion as a kid (Judges 14:6). He came upon [[Saul]] at his anointing, turning him into "another man" (1 Samuel 10:6), and departed from him after his disobedience (1 Samuel 16:14) — the same chapter in which the Spirit came upon [[David]] "from that day forward" (16:13). David's plea after his sin with Bathsheba — "take not thy holy spirit from me" (Psalm 51:11) — reflects the OT pattern: the Spirit's presence with an individual was not yet the settled, permanent indwelling that the New Testament promises to every believer.

The Spirit also inspired prophetic speech: "The Spirit of the LORD spake by me, and his word was in my tongue" (2 Samuel 23:2, David's last words). Micah could say, "I am full of power by the spirit of the LORD" (Micah 3:8) to declare Israel's sin. Peter later summarizes the whole phenomenon: "holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost" (2 Peter 1:21) — the Spirit as the ultimate author behind every human writer of Scripture.

## The Prophetic Promise

The prophets look forward to a coming day when the Spirit's work would no longer be occasional and selective but universal and internal. Ezekiel promises a new heart: "A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you... And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:26-27) — the Spirit not merely acting upon a person from outside but dwelling within, producing obedience from the inside out. Joel promises the Spirit poured out broadly, beyond kings and prophets: "I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions: And also upon the servants and upon the handmaids in those days will I pour out my spirit" (Joel 2:28-29) — a promise that erases the old boundaries of who could receive the Spirit's empowering presence. Isaiah anticipates the Spirit resting fully on the coming Messiah: "the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD" (Isaiah 11:2), and the Servant's own words, "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek" (Isaiah 61:1) — the very words [[Jesus Christ]] read aloud in the Nazareth synagogue and applied to himself (Luke 4:18-21).

## The Spirit and Jesus Christ

The Spirit is present at every major turning point of the Son's earthly life. [[Mary (Mother of Jesus)]] conceives by the Spirit's overshadowing power: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). At the [[Baptism of Jesus]], the Spirit descends "like a dove" and lights upon him (Matthew 3:16) — the Father's anointing of the Son for public ministry, witnessed by all three persons of the Godhead at once. The Spirit then drives him into the wilderness for the [[Temptation of Jesus]] (Mark 1:12), and he returns "in the power of the Spirit" to begin his Galilean ministry (Luke 4:14), casting out demons "by the Spirit of God" as proof that "the kingdom of God is come unto you" (Matthew 12:28).

In the [[Farewell Discourse]] on the night before his death, Jesus promises his disciples that his going away is to their advantage, because only then will he send "another Comforter" — the Greek *allos parakletos*, another of the same kind as himself — to be with them forever (John 14:16). This Comforter is "the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you" (John 14:17). He will teach the disciples all things and bring to remembrance everything Jesus said (14:26); he will testify of Jesus (15:26); and when he comes he "will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment" (16:8) and "will guide you into all truth... he shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you" (16:13-14). The Spirit's mission is bound to the Son's mission — sent by the Father in the Son's name (14:26), sent by the Son from the Father (15:26), and always oriented toward making the Son known rather than himself.

## Pentecost and the Church

Fifty days after the resurrection, the promise Jesus gave before his ascension — "ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence" (Acts 1:5), and "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me" (Acts 1:8) — was fulfilled. "There appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance" (Acts 2:3-4). [[Peter]] explained the event as the direct fulfillment of Joel's prophecy (Acts 2:16-21) — the age of the Spirit poured out on all flesh had begun. From this point, the settled, permanent indwelling of every believer becomes the ordinary New Testament experience rather than the occasional empowering seen under the old covenant: "Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?" (1 Corinthians 3:16); "if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his" (Romans 8:9).

The book of Acts records the Spirit as the active agent driving the church's mission — directing [[Philip (Evangelist)|Philip]] to the Ethiopian eunuch (Acts 8:29), setting apart [[Paul (Apostle)|Paul]] and [[Barnabas]] for the first missionary journey (Acts 13:2), and forbidding Paul to preach in Asia and Bithynia so that the gospel would go instead into Europe (Acts 16:6-7). The Spirit is not a passive presence but an active director of the church's history.

## The Spirit's Work in Believers

**Regeneration and New Birth:** "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God... That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" (John 3:5-6). The new birth is his work, not a human achievement — "he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost" (Titus 3:5).

**Indwelling and Sealing:** Every believer is permanently indwelt: "ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you" (Romans 8:9). He is given as a "seal" and an "earnest" — a down payment guaranteeing the fullness to come: "ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession" (Ephesians 1:13-14).

**Sanctification and Fruit:** He produces Christlike character over time: "the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance" (Galatians 5:22-23) — a single "fruit," not fruits, describing one unified character rather than a checklist of separate virtues. Believers are commanded to "walk in the Spirit" (5:16) and are progressively transformed "from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

**Gifts and Empowerment:** He distributes spiritual gifts for the common good — wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment, tongues, and interpretation — "dividing to every man severally as he will" (1 Corinthians 12:8-11), so that the body of Christ is built up in unity and diversity together (12:12-13).

**Intercession:** "the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered" (Romans 8:26) when believers do not know how to pray as they ought — the Spirit praying within and for the saints "according to the will of God" (8:27).

**Assurance:** "The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God" (Romans 8:16), and by him believers cry "Abba, Father" (8:15) — the intimate address of a child, not a servant.

## Personhood and Deity

The Spirit is consistently treated in Scripture as a person, not a force. He can be grieved: "grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption" (Ephesians 4:30). He can be lied to, as Ananias discovered fatally — Peter's charge, "why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost?" is immediately restated as "thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God" (Acts 5:3-4), one of the clearest single statements of the Spirit's full deity in Scripture. He teaches (John 14:26), testifies (15:26), convicts (16:8), guides (16:13), speaks (Acts 13:2), forbids (Acts 16:6), and can be resisted (Acts 7:51) and blasphemed (Matthew 12:31-32) — all personal, relational actions, never merely mechanical ones.

His deity is affirmed directly: lying to him is lying to God (Acts 5:3-4); he searches "the deep things of God," just as a man's own spirit alone knows the things of a man (1 Corinthians 2:10-11); and the baptismal formula places him on equal footing with the Father and the Son — "baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 28:19), one name shared by three persons. Paul's benediction closes 2 Corinthians the same way: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all" (2 Corinthians 13:14) — Son, Father, and Spirit named together as the one source of the church's blessing.

**The Unforgivable Sin:** Jesus warns of a singular exception to the otherwise universal offer of forgiveness — "the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men... it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come" (Matthew 12:31-32). Spoken in response to the Pharisees attributing his Spirit-empowered exorcisms to Satan, this warns against a settled, willful rejection of the Spirit's testimony to Christ — not a single doubt or blasphemous word spoken in weakness, which Scripture elsewhere treats as forgivable, but the hardened refusal to receive the very testimony by which anyone comes to repentance at all.

## Theological Significance

**Full Deity, Distinct Person:** The Spirit is not a mode of the Father, an impersonal energy the Father and Son emit, or a lesser being subordinate in nature. He is the third person of the one Godhead — fully God, eternally distinct from the Father and the Son, yet undivided from them in essence. The historic doctrine of the Trinity rests as much on the Spirit's personhood and deity as on the Son's.

**Procession and Mission:** He proceeds from the Father (John 15:26) and is sent by the Son (16:7) — a relationship of origin, not a difference of rank. His mission in redemptive history follows the Son's: promised through the prophets, present at the Son's conception and anointing, poured out fully only after the Son's ascension, so that the age of the Spirit is inseparably the age of the exalted Christ's presence with his church.

**The Down Payment of the Age to Come:** His indwelling is described as "firstfruits" (Romans 8:23) and "earnest" (2 Corinthians 1:22; 5:5) — not merely a comforting presence in the present but a guarantee, a partial and certain foretaste of the resurrection life believers will fully receive when Christ returns. "he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:6) is, in substance, a promise about the Spirit's ongoing work.

**Christ-Centered, Not Self-Centered:** His characteristic work is to exalt the Son rather than himself — "he shall glorify me" (John 16:14) — which is why Scripture gives comparatively little independent biography to the Spirit relative to the Father and the Son: his very mission is to be the one through whom the Father and the Son are known, present, and active, rather than the direct object of that knowledge himself.
